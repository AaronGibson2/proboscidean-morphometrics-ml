# M3/m3 morphology: primary-literature evidence and a review protocol

Research date: 23 September 2026. This is a literature note, not a new identification of any project specimen. Capital **M3** means upper third molar; lowercase **m3** means lower third molar. The comparison concerns genera first: *Gomphotherium* is a genus, whereas *Amebelodon floridanus* is a named species whose generic placement is disputed.

**Useful outcome:** test combinations of observable anatomical characters against documented reference teeth. Neither locality nor DINOv3 similarity supplies a taxonomic identification. Keep an unresolved category instead of forcing every tooth into one of two groups.

## 1. What the primary papers actually establish

### The current name of the Florida animal is disputed

Jukar, Millhouse and Carrano (2025), *Identity of the trilophodont gomphothere from Mixson’s Bone Bed, Florida*, JVP 44(4), e2453301, argue that the original descriptive material is lost, designate a neotype from Mixson’s, and retain the species in *Amebelodon*. Their abstract describes its dentition as relatively primitive compared with *A. fricki* and emphasizes distinguishing lower-tusk characters. **Access limitation:** the publisher abstract and bibliographic record were accessible; the full article was not obtained in this review. No detailed M3 measurements or figure descriptions are attributed to that inaccessible text. [Publisher abstract and DOI](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02724634.2025.2453301); [Smithsonian publication record](https://research.si.edu/publication-details/?id=175112).

Lambert (2025), *The validity of the genus Stenobelodon…*, New Mexico Museum of Natural History and Science Bulletin **101:213–218**, contests that treatment and favors *Stenobelodon floridanus*. His morphological comparison emphasizes serridentine subdivision, limited/weak posttrite trefoiling, and relatively narrow, modestly flattened lower tusks, contrasting with *A. fricki*. The latter has stronger posttrite trefoiling extending farther along the crown and lacks the serridentine condition in his diagnosis. Crucially, his discussion identifies *A. hicksi* as sharing the dental combination, with tusks supplying a distinction. Fig. 2 illustrates USNM 3066 m2/m3; the paper argues that this is the legitimate type, replacing the status assigned to USNM 3083 in Lambert (2023). This note reports the competing interpretations without adjudicating nomenclatural rules. [Author-uploaded full text, especially pp. 214–217 and Fig. 2](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396476723_THE_VALIDITY_OF_THE_GENUS_STENOBELODON_PROBOSCIDEA_GOMPHOTHERIIDAE_A_RESPONSE_TO_THE_REFERRAL_OF_THE_MIXSON%27S_BONE_BED_LATE_MIOCENE_FLORIDA_GOMPHOTHERE_TO_AMEBELODON).

For project labels, use **“floridanus comparison group; Amebelodon/Stenobelodon generic placement disputed”** until an adopted taxonomic treatment is documented. That is a project convention, not a proposed taxonomic act.

### Direct floridanus reference molars and a critical ridge-count exception

Lambert (2023), JVP 43(1), e2252021, provides these directly relevant comparisons:

| Reference | Published observation or measurement | Locator |
|---|---|---|
| USNM 3083, lower m3 | Five lophids, no clear talonid; nearly unworn; weak double trefoiling; 226 × 85 mm | pp. 3–4, Fig. 3D, Table 1 |
| USNM 3084, upper M3 | Four strong lophs plus a weak fifth **alternatively interpreted as a talon**; 190 × 102 mm | pp. 4–5, Fig. 3E, Table 1 |
| Tyner catalog check | UF 212304 listed with m1/m2; UF 212305 and UF 217472 include third molars | p. 2, Included Specimens |

Serridentine structure is cusp subdivision into serrated crests; wear complicates interpretation. *Serbelodon barbourensis* has conservative, *Gomphotherium*-like cheek teeth. **A four-versus-five binary rule fails for the floridanus reference above.** Catalog inconsistencies also require checking: Table 1 prints UNSM where descriptions use USNM; the Tyner M1 is UF 217473 in the description/table but UF 322684 in Fig. 3A. Verify the photographed tooth position against specimen records. [Lambert 2023, full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/374413355_Implications_of_discoveries_of_the_shovel-tusked_gomphothere_Konobelodon_Proboscidea_Gomphotheriidae_in_Eurasia_for_the_status_of_Amebelodon_with_a_new_genus_of_shovel-tusked_gomphothere_Stenobelodon); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/02724634.2023.2252021).

### Orientation and why “trilophodont” does not mean a three-ridged M3

Lambert (2007), JVP **27:676–682**, Fig. 1, illustrates the conservative *Gomphotherium* upper-molar pattern: worn trefoils are concentrated on the pretrite side. **Pretrite is lingual in upper teeth, labial/buccal in lower teeth.** His discussion of *Tetralophodon* makes clear that the defining four-ridge condition concerns intermediate cheek teeth, not simply the count on an isolated terminal molar. The paper erects *Pediolophodon* for North American material previously referred to *Tetralophodon*, using the distribution of three versus four ridges along the tooth series. Consequently, an isolated many-ridged M3 cannot alone establish *Tetralophodon*. [Full text, p. 676 Fig. 1, diagnosis/discussion pp. 677–681](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232665893_New_tetralophodont_gomphothere_material_from_Nebraska_and_its_implications_for_the_status_of_North_American_Tetralophodon).

### Gomphotherium itself varies: five ridges and serration are not exclusive

Tassy (2014), Geodiversitas **36:35–115**, documents variation in a substantial European *G. angustidens* sample from En Péjouan. In the abstract’s anatomical synthesis, upper M3 have four lophs; lower m3 have four **or five** lophids. Central conules may be subdivided into a serridentine pattern or remain simple, including variation between left and right teeth of one individual. Posttrite conules are weak or absent. This is a comparative demonstration of overlap, **not evidence that Florida teeth belong to the European species**. [Full author text and abstract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/273009490_L%27odontologie_de_Gomphotherium_angustidens_Cuvier_1817_Proboscidea_Mammalia_donnees_issues_du_gisement_d%27En_Pejouan_Miocene_moyen_du_Gers_France); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5252/g2014n1a2).

Wang, Zhang and Li (2020), Vertebrata PalAsiatica **58:134–158**, independently illustrate North American *G. productum* type-cast AMNH 14383 in Fig. 4L–N. Their comparison describes strong central conules that can themselves subdivide into serrations, relatively narrow interlophid spaces, and substantial enamel pillars visible from the side. Fig. 5 and Tables S1–S3 provide a model for explicitly defined proportion measurements rather than visual impressions of “narrowness.” The study addresses mammutid/gomphothere relationships; these features are comparative observations, not a validated classifier for this Florida dataset. [Full text, comparative section and Figs. 4–5](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/340643777_Reappraisal_of_Serridentinus_gobiensis_Osborn_Granger_and_Miomastodon_tongxinensis_Chen_the_validity_of_Miomastodon); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.19615/j.cnki.1000-3118.200310).

### Alternatives worth comparing, without treating them as equally likely

**Konobelodon britti:** Lambert (1990), Journal of Paleontology **64:1032–1040**, describes the Florida Moss Acres holotype UF 70000. Its elongated lower third molars have six lophids and small talonids. The original description distinguishes highly trefoiled ectolophids from only incipiently trefoiled entolophids; this should prevent an oversimplified “every loph is strongly double-trefoiled” rule. Diagnosis also involves intermediate cheek teeth and lower-tusk internal structure. Table 1 gives measurements, and the species description begins around pp. 1034–1035. [Author full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/27393868_Rediagnosis_of_the_genus_Amebelodon_Mammalia_Proboscidea_Gomphotheriidae_with_a_new_subgenus_and_species_Amebelodon_Konobelodon_britti); [publisher/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022336000019855).

**Other shovel-tuskers:** Lambert (2016), JVP e1091352, distinguishes *Eurybelodon shoshanii* using a combination of weakly single-trefoiled molars, four m3 lophids with a weak posterior element, and unusual tusks with corrugated enamel. This is an Oregon comparator, not a proposed Florida identification. It is a useful counterexample to identifying shovel-tusker relationships from a generic-looking molar alone. [Author full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/295398216_Eurybelodon_shoshanii_an_unusual_new_shovel-tusked_gomphothere_Mammalia_Proboscidea_from_the_late_Miocene_of_Oregon); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/02724634.2016.1091352).

**An explicit recent example of restraint:** Eshelman, Lambert, Morgan, Godfrey and Bohaska (2026), chapter 1 of Smithsonian Contributions to Paleobiology **109**, discuss Chesapeake teeth with possible *Stenobelodon* affinity while retaining uncertainty because associated second molars, mandibles and tusks are absent. Relevant comparison plates include Fig. 1.23 (USNM V 25873), Fig. 1.25, and Fig. 1.28 (*G. productum* USNM V 2346). Their Table 1.2 lists tooth dimensions. This demonstrates a reasonable outcome for an isolated molar: documented resemblance without a definitive species referral. [Author-uploaded chapter, especially comparative discussion and conclusions](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/404162426_Miocene_Chesapeake_Gomphotheriidae_sfs).

## 2. A proposed scoring sheet for our images

The following is a **project proposal**, not a scoring system already validated in the cited papers. It turns their recurring comparisons into reviewable observations. Score upper and lower teeth separately. Do not translate a photographic left/right position into lingual/buccal until the anatomical orientation is verified.

| Field | What to record | How to handle uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| Tooth position and side | Verified M3/m3, left/right, anterior/posterior, pretrite/posttrite | Preserve the filename assertion separately from reviewed anatomy |
| Crown completeness | Missing anterior, posterior or side portions; obscuring matrix | A broken posterior crown has a minimum count, not a complete count |
| Main ridge count | Number of identifiable complete lophs/lophids | Record a count interval if a ridge boundary is ambiguous |
| Posterior structure | Separate talon/talonid; possible weak terminal ridge | Preserve competing interpretations, such as “4 + talon / weak fifth” |
| Ridge-by-ridge cusp arrangement | Mark principal cusp and accessory cusps on both sides | Do not infer cusps merely from dark staining or cracks |
| Pretrite trefoiling | Presence and form on each interpretable ridge | Separate unworn cusp arrangement from an actual worn trefoil outline |
| Posttrite complexity | Absent, incipient, or developed; extent along crown | Require the reviewer to mark the structures supporting the score |
| Cusp subdivision | Which principal/accessory cusps are subdivided | Keep structural subdivision separate from enamel surface wrinkles |
| Crown proportions | Length; maximum width; width of each complete ridge; posterior taper | Millimetres require calibrated scale; retain perspective limitations |
| Wear | Score per ridge, with photographs | Unobservable because worn is different from anatomically absent |
| Associated material | Jaw, M2/m2, tusks, specimen/catalog links | Do not assume separate teeth at one site belong to the same animal |
| Identification evidence | Compatible features, contradictory features, unresolved features | Keep observation, taxonomic interpretation and reference source separate |

For the first pass, hide locality and existing proposed taxon from anatomical reviewers. Use at least two independent scorings for the difficult traits, reconcile differences, and retain the initial disagreement. A reference plate should include more than one tooth and wear stage for each comparison group; a single famous type is not a measured population range.

## 3. What a defensible result would look like

Use these as report categories, not as a numerical probability model:

1. **Morphologically compatible with a named reference group:** several interpretable characters agree, with the supporting regions marked.
2. **Compatible with multiple groups:** the observable characters overlap and additional material is needed.
3. **Contradicts the initial reference group:** one or more clearly preserved features disagree; show the evidence and expand comparison.
4. **Insufficient preservation or views:** an identification cannot be evaluated from the available image.

A difference from two reference specimens does not establish a new species. Before interpreting an outlier taxonomically, check tooth position, orientation, wear, damage, within-species variation, reference coverage and chronology. The source papers supply examples of these ambiguities; this sequence is the proposed project safeguard against confusing an image outlier with a biological discovery.

DINOv3 can assist by retrieving comparable photographs **after** anatomical regions are reviewed. The anatomical scoring and reference comparison should carry the taxonomic argument. Existing image-feature clusters should remain a separate exploratory result and should not be reused as “verified species” labels.
